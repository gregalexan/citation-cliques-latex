Manuscript QSS-2026-0077.R1 - Major Revision

Title: Citation-Network Cohesion and Reciprocal Citation Cliques Across Journal-Impact Groups: A Matched Author Analysis

Dear Dr. Larivière,

Thank you for the opportunity to revise the manuscript again, and thank you to Reviewer 1 for a second careful reading. The reviewer is right that our previous revision answered the first-round concerns about rigour by adding technical material to the main text, and that the scientific message was lost as a result. This revision is a restructuring rather than a rewording.

The principal changes are:

1. Structure and technical detail. All implementation material (venue-score equations, construction and missing-value rules, graph notation and formulas, the matching audit, the screen specification, reproducibility checks, and the journal-classification details) has moved to appendices after the references. The main text has one compact Data and Methods section without equations, and the eight measures are described in plain language. The first-round corrections (matching rule and order, named paired test, Control-percentile screen, sensitivity analyses, formal clique definition) remain in force and are summarized in the main text.

2. Narrative and interpretation. The Introduction now starts from the conceptual problem that citation cohesion can reflect specialization, collaboration, regional communities, or strategic exchange. A new Background section reviews theories of citing, collaboration and self-citation, citation cartels, invisible colleges and topical proximity, journal stratification, and field normalization. The Discussion interprets the findings against this literature, with a new figure that sets out the competing explanations and what the data can examine, and adds implications for scientometric practice.

3. Motivation for methodological choices. Each choice (Eigenfactor groups and cutoffs, the 70% rule, h5 matching, the caliper, clique thresholds, screen inputs and percentiles) is now motivated where it is introduced, together with its sensitivity analysis. A redrawn study-design figure and a new figure illustrating the clique thresholds support the methods summary.

4. Field differences and collaboration. Clique membership is now reported by subject, and a new analysis repeats the clique comparison after removing citation links between prior coauthors. Team structure and topical proximity are discussed as explanations and stated as limitations.

5. Tables. Every table states its denominators and explains its calculations in words. Former Table 12 has been rewritten accordingly, the mixing table now reports shares, and subjects are named. Main-text tables were reduced from eleven to six.

6. Limitations. The section now begins with conceptual limitations: the meaning of a reciprocal clique, specialization and topical proximity, journal-impact group as a bundle of characteristics, the boundary of the matched-author graph, and the associational design.

A detailed point-by-point response, a table mapping each part of the previous version to its new location, the clean manuscript with its appendices, and a marked manuscript are provided.
