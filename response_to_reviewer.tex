Dear Dr.~Larivi\`ere,

Thank you for the opportunity to revise the manuscript again, and thank you to Reviewer~1 for a second careful reading.  The reviewer's message is clear: the previous version answered the first-round concerns about rigour by adding technical material to the main text, and the scientific message was lost.  We agree.  This revision is a restructuring rather than a rewording.  The main text now presents concepts, reasoning, results, and their interpretation in the context of the scientometric literature; implementation detail has moved to appendices after the references.  Although the Background and Discussion are now considerably longer, the main text is shorter overall, and its methods section is about half its former length.

All first-round corrections remain in force: the explicit matching rule and order, the named paired test, the Control-percentile screen without feature weights or normality assumptions, the sensitivity analyses, and the formal clique definition.  Each is now summarized in one or two plain sentences in the main text and documented in full in the appendices.  Table~\ref{tab:map} shows where each element of the previous version now appears.

Our responses to the reviewer's comments follow.  References to sections, tables, figures, and appendices are to the revised manuscript.

\section*{Comment 1: Overall assessment}

\commenttext{I confirm that the paper addresses an interesting question: whether authors publishing in lower-impact journals show different citation patterns compared to similar authors publishing in higher-impact journals. The topic is relevant for scientometrics, and the dataset is large. However, the manuscript is very difficult to read in its current form. The writing is extremely technical, the structure is hard to follow, and the discussion is minimal. As a result, the scientific message is not clear.}

\response We restructured the paper around its scientific message.  The Introduction now opens with the conceptual problem (citation cohesion can reflect specialization, collaboration, regional communities, or strategic exchange) and explains why comparing journal-impact groups is informative.  A new Background section reviews the relevant literature in four themes.  Methods are presented in one compact section.  Results are organized by research question, each subsection opening with its main finding.  The Discussion interprets the findings against the literature and draws implications for practice.

\location{Abstract; Sections~\ref{ms-sec:introduction}--\ref{ms-sec:conclusion}}

\section*{Comment 2: The manuscript is overly technical}

\commenttext{Large parts of the paper read more like software documentation than a scientometric article. Many pages are devoted to implementation details, internal checks, matching audits. These details overwhelm the reader and distract from the scientific questions.}

\response We moved all implementation material to appendices after the references: the venue-score equations, the reference-resolution and fractional-weight rules, graph notation, measure formulas, zero and missing-value rules, the matching audit, the screen specification, the reproducibility checks, and the journal-classification details.  We also removed sentences that responded to first-round comments inside the manuscript (for example, statements about what the analysis does \emph{not} do), which had made the text read like a change log.  The main text now has one Data and Methods section with five short subsections and no equations; the eight measures are described in plain language in Table~\ref{ms-tab:measures}, and their formulas are in Table~\ref{ms-tab:metric-definitions}.  A redrawn study-design diagram (Figure~\ref{ms-fig:study-design}) gives the overall logic before any detail.

\location{Section~\ref{ms-sec:methods}; Table~\ref{ms-tab:measures}; Appendices~\ref{ms-app:venue-scores}--\ref{ms-app:checks}}

\section*{Comment 3: Lack of narrative and interpretation}

\commenttext{The paper presents many definitions, metrics, and tables, but offers very little interpretation. The ``Discussion'' section mostly repeats numerical results without explaining what they mean, why they matter, or how they relate to existing scientometric literature. Readers are left without a clear understanding of the implications.}

\response The Discussion no longer repeats numbers.  It is organized around what the results mean: (i) what cohesion can mean under normative and social-constructivist theories of citing; (ii) the role of collaboration and teams; (iii) field differences; (iv) why both groups cite mainly within themselves, and why this is partly expected by construction; (v) how the findings relate to journal-level cartel detection and author-level concentration indicators; and (vi) implications for scientometric practice.  We also interpret patterns that the previous version reported without comment, such as the lower same-journal referencing of Cases and the fact that most authors in both groups have no cohesive ties.  Each Discussion paragraph is tied to work reviewed in the Background, and the bibliography now includes the relevant literature on citing motivations, collaboration and self-citation, citation cartels, invisible colleges and topical proximity, journal stratification, field normalization, team science, and responsible metrics.

\location{Sections~\ref{ms-sec:background} and~\ref{ms-sec:discussion}}

\section*{Comment 4: Missing motivation for methodological choices}

\commenttext{Key decisions (e.g., h5 matching, caliper = 3, clique thresholds, percentile cutoffs, feature selection for the screen) are presented without explanation. It is unclear why these choices are appropriate or how sensitive the results are to alternative specifications.}

\response Each choice is now motivated where it is introduced, and its sensitivity is stated.  A new diagram (Figure~\ref{ms-fig:clique-concept}) shows what the density and reciprocity thresholds of a reciprocal clique mean with a four-author example:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Eigenfactor and 25th/75th percentile cutoffs:} a citation-flow score that excludes journal self-citation, which matters for a study of closed citation patterns; the cutoffs leave the middle half of output out so the groups are clearly separated, and they are computed within subject because citation levels differ by field.  We also note that the score grows with journal size, which helps interpret the results.
  \item \emph{70\% portfolio rule:} assigns authors whose output is mostly in one group while allowing some publication elsewhere.
  \item \emph{$h_5$ matching:} authors with more citations have more links and more chances to show any network pattern, so we match on a subject-specific $h$-index measured in the same 2020--2024 window as the outcomes.
  \item \emph{Caliper of three:} an upper bound that was rarely needed; \ExactHFivePairPercent\% of pairs have identical $h_5$, and no pair differs by more than \MaximumAbsoluteHFiveDifference.  Repeating the analysis on exact matches leaves all primary results unchanged in direction.
  \item \emph{Clique thresholds:} four is the smallest group that is not a single triangle; 75\% density allows a few missing links; 50\% reciprocity means exchange is at least as much two-way as one-way.  The result has the same direction under all nine combinations of size and reciprocity threshold.
  \item \emph{Screen inputs and percentile cutoffs:} the five author-link measures of cohesion and concentration; the other three measures use different populations or time.  Control percentiles avoid normality assumptions.  The result is stable across thresholds, seeds, and the removal of any single input.
\end{itemize}

\location{Sections~\ref{ms-sec:data}--\ref{ms-sec:analysis}; Appendices~\ref{ms-app:paired}--\ref{ms-app:screen}}

\section*{Comment 5: Very limited connection to scientometric practice}

\commenttext{The paper does not discuss: field differences, collaboration patterns, team structure, topical proximity, and other questions relevant for a QSS reader and central for interpreting results.}

\response We address each point with evidence where the data allow and with explicit discussion where they do not.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Field differences:} clique membership is now reported by subject (Figure~\ref{ms-fig:clique-membership} and Table~\ref{ms-tab:clique-subjects}).  Cases have the higher rate in \CliqueSubjectsCaseHigher\ of \CliqueSubjectCount\ subjects, but the size of the gap varies widely.  The Discussion relates this to field differences in citation practice and argues that cohesion should be compared within field.
  \item \emph{Collaboration patterns:} we added an analysis that repeats the clique comparison after removing all citation links between prior coauthors (\CliqueNoCoauthorCaseMembershipPercent\% of Cases versus \CliqueNoCoauthorControlMembershipPercent\% of Controls remain in reciprocal cliques).  This shows how much of the pattern depends on collaboration.  We also interpret the prior-coauthor citation results, which show that most Cases rarely cite prior collaborators while a minority cite them heavily.
  \item \emph{Team structure:} discussed in relation to the literature on team science and collaboration networks, and listed as an unbalanced characteristic in the Limitations.
  \item \emph{Topical proximity:} the five broad subjects cannot measure it.  We discuss it as the most important alternative explanation for cohesion, citing evidence that citation is more likely between topically close papers, and state it as a conceptual limitation.
\end{itemize}
A new figure in the Discussion (Figure~\ref{ms-fig:explanations}) sets out four explanations for cohesive structure (specialization, regional and language communities, stable teams, and strategic exchange) and states which of them our data can examine.  A new paragraph on implications for scientometric practice discusses within-field comparison of cohesion, cohesion as context rather than verdict, screening as a way to prioritize qualitative review, and the risk of using journal-impact group as a proxy for individual behavior.

\location{Sections~\ref{ms-sec:clique-results}, \ref{ms-sec:discussion}, and~\ref{ms-sec:limitations}; Table~\ref{ms-tab:clique-summary}}

\section*{Comment 6: Difficult presentation of results}

\commenttext{Several tables (e.g., Table 12) are extremely hard to understand. Denominators are not clearly stated, and the logic of the calculations is not explained. This makes it difficult for readers to verify or interpret the findings.}

\response We revised every table.  Each now states its denominator in the column header or note and explains each calculation in words.  Former Table~12 (screen stability when one input is removed) is now Table~\ref{ms-tab:anomaly-feature-ablation} in Appendix~\ref{ms-app:screen}: its percentages are labeled ``\% of eligible'' with the eligible counts given in the note, ``Enrichment'' is replaced by ``Case/Control ratio'' with its formula, and the overlap measure is explained in words.  The citation-mixing table now reports the share of each group's citations going to each group, instead of raw fractional weights, and compares the observed within-group share with the share expected from the totals.  The paired-comparison tables were reduced to the columns needed for interpretation.  Subjects are named rather than numbered.  Main-text tables were reduced from eleven to six, and sensitivity tables moved to the appendices.

\location{Tables~\ref{ms-tab:matching-balance}--\ref{ms-tab:group-mixing}; Tables~\ref{ms-tab:paired-secondary}--\ref{ms-tab:anomaly-feature-ablation}}

\section*{Comment 7: Minimal discussion of limitations}

\commenttext{Although a limitations section exists, it focuses mostly on technical constraints. Broader conceptual limitations, such as the meaning of ``reciprocal cliques'' or the role of specialization, are not addressed.}

\response The Limitations section now begins with conceptual limits: a reciprocal clique is a pattern of links that specialized research fronts, long-term collaborations, and agreed exchange can all produce; topical proximity and specialization are not measured; journal-impact group bundles journal size, audience, language, region, and editorial practice; peer-based measures describe how matched authors cite each other, not the full network; and the design is associational.  Data and measurement limits follow briefly.  The meaning of a reciprocal clique is also discussed where the concept is introduced and in the Discussion.

\location{Sections~\ref{ms-sec:cliques}, \ref{ms-sec:discussion}, and~\ref{ms-sec:limitations}}

\section*{Comment 8: Overall suggestion}

\commenttext{I suggest to the authors to reduce technical details and strengthen the narrative of the papers in order to communicate their results to QSS readers. To this end it is necessary that the main text is focused on concepts, reasoning, and interpretation of the results in the context of scientometric literature.}

\response We followed this suggestion throughout, as described above.  The findings themselves are unchanged except for the added collaboration and subject analyses: Cases were more cohesive on all primary measures and more often belonged to reciprocal citation cliques (\CliqueCaseMembershipPercent\% versus \CliqueControlMembershipPercent\%), while most authors in both groups had no such ties.  The paper now presents these results as evidence about the structure of research communities, which structural data alone cannot attribute to a single mechanism.
