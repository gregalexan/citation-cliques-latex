\begin{abstract}
We examine whether author citation-network structure differs between low- and high-impact journal strata after matching authors on subject and recent citation productivity.  Crossref records from 2020--2024 are used to classify journals by subject-specific Eigenfactor quartiles and to construct \MatchedPairCount\ matched Case--Control author pairs.  Citation weights are fractionalized by the complete author counts of both works, while graph endpoints require identified authors.  Four cohesion and concentration measures are primary outcomes; four additional measures are secondary.  Pairwise inference uses complete pairs for each outcome, paired bootstrap intervals, sign-flip tests, and within-family multiplicity correction.  A separate Control-referenced Isolation Forest screen identifies unusual author--subject profiles and describes qualifying connected components without inferring intent.  \PrimaryFindingText\ \AnomalyFindingText\ The design distinguishes outgoing author neighborhoods from the matched-author induced graph and treats unavailable measurements as missing.  The results characterize associations between venue strata and citation structure; they do not establish coordination, manipulation, or misconduct.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} scientometrics; citation networks; matched design; network cohesion; journal impact; anomaly screening

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Citation indicators are widely used to summarize scholarly influence, allocate resources, and support evaluation.  Their convenience also creates incentives to optimize what is measured, which can weaken the connection between an indicator and the quality it is intended to represent \parencite{Goo84,Hicks2015,Biagioli2019}.  Excessive self-citation, coercive citation, and concentrated citation exchange are therefore important objects of measurement in their own right \parencite{Wilhite2012,VanNoorden2019,Biagioli2020}.

Most evidence on anomalous citation structure is either journal-level or begins with groups already selected for unusual behavior.  Journal-network studies can identify exceptional exchange patterns \parencite{Kojaku2021}, and analyses of citation stacking show how journal-level citation kinetics can be distorted \parencite{heneberg2016}.  Author-level concentration indicators, in turn, can reveal when a small set of collaborators accounts for a large share of an author's citation impact \parencite{Evdaimon2024}.  Less is known about whether interpretable author-network measures differ across journal-impact strata when authors are compared within subject and at similar levels of recent citation productivity.

This study addresses that gap with a matched author--subject design.  Low-impact and high-impact refer only to the bottom and top subject-specific quartiles of the journal Eigenfactor distribution.  We use \emph{Case} and \emph{Control} as compact labels for those operational strata; neither label evaluates the scientific merit of an author or work.

The study asks three questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Do matched Case and Control authors differ in citation-network cohesion and outgoing citation concentration?
  \item Are author--subject profiles that are unusual relative to Controls enriched among Cases?
  \item What is the descriptive structure of connected components formed by flagged profiles, and how do fractional citation weights mix across the two tiers?
\end{enumerate}

The questions are associational.  Network closure may reflect many mechanisms, including legitimate topical specialization and collaboration.  Accordingly, the anomaly procedure is presented as a screening device, and component labels describe graph position rather than motive or conduct.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

Research evaluation based on citation counts, the $h$-index, and journal indicators has long been accompanied by cautions about field differences and strategic responses \parencite{Gar06,Hirsch2005,Hicks2015}.  Self-citation is common and can be substantively appropriate, but extreme rates complicate comparisons \parencite{Aksnes2003,VanNoorden2019}.  Coercive citation and metric gaming demonstrate that observed references can also respond to editorial and institutional incentives \parencite{Wilhite2012,Baccini2019}.

Several strands of work motivate a network perspective.  Kojaku et al.\ identify anomalous groups in journal citation networks by comparing observed patterns with network expectations \parencite{Kojaku2021}.  Heneberg examines the transition from journal self-citation to citation stacking through journal-level citation flows \parencite{heneberg2016}.  These approaches establish the value of relational structure but operate at the venue level.  At the author level, Evdaimon et al.\ propose indicators based on the concentration of citing authors and hyper-collaboration \parencite{Evdaimon2024}.  Their concentration measures identify a narrow citing base; local closure, reciprocity, and concentration of an author's own references provide complementary information about how that base is connected.

The present analysis combines this network perspective with within-subject author matching.  It does not attempt to classify behavior as legitimate or illegitimate.  Instead, it estimates matched differences in a small, declared set of network measures and applies a Control-referenced screen whose output remains explicitly descriptive.

\section{Data and Cohort}
\label{sec:data-cohort}

\subsection{Source data and study window}

The source is a fixed Crossref snapshot processed with \textit{alexandria3k} \parencite{Spinellis2023}.  The study window covers citing and cited works dated 2020--2024.  Available fields include DOI, publication year, normalized journal ISSN, work references, and author rows with optional ORCID identifiers.  Crossref coverage and ORCID availability are incomplete; these limitations affect which citation endpoints can enter an author graph (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

Journals are assigned to Health Sciences, Life Sciences, Physical Sciences, Social Sciences and Humanities, or Multidisciplinary.  The existing classification uses a deterministic, cached large-language-model annotation of representative journal content.  Within each subject, journal influence is measured with Eigenfactor on the 2020--2024 journal citation network, excluding journal self-citations \parencite{West2008}.  Journals below the 25th percentile form the low-impact stratum and those above the 75th percentile form the high-impact stratum.  No new venue classification was undertaken for this revision.

\subsection{Author--subject cohort}

The unit used throughout is an \emph{author--subject}, identified by $(\mathrm{ORCID},s)$.  An author is a Case in subject $s$ when at least 70\% of eligible works in that subject appear in low-impact journals, and a Control when at least 70\% appear in high-impact journals.  Authors not meeting either rule are excluded from matching.  A uniqueness check prevents more than one tier assignment for the same $(\mathrm{ORCID},s)$ key.

The retained primary cohort contains \MatchedPairCount\ matched Case--Control pairs across the five subjects.  The deterministic 10\% ORCID sample and the original matching cohort are retained to separate analytical corrections from cohort redefinition.

\section{Matching}
\label{sec:matching}

For each subject, Case authors were matched without replacement to Control authors on subject-specific $h_5$, using the existing candidate-caliper and nearest-match procedure.  The matching table supplies the Case ORCID, Control ORCID, subject, and both $h_5$ values.  Feature rows are linked back to this table twice, on $(\mathrm{case\_orcid},s)$ and $(\mathrm{control\_orcid},s)$; ORCID-only joins are not used.

We report the distribution of $h_5$ and the within-pair difference before outcome testing.  Because residual differences can remain under caliper matching, the four primary comparisons are repeated on the subset with exact $h_5$ equality.

\section{Network Construction and Definitions}
\label{sec:network-construction}

\subsection{Reference resolution and fractional weights}

A citing-work/cited-work DOI pair is deduplicated before either work is expanded to author rows.  Let $n_c$ and $n_a$ be the complete counts of author rows on the citing and cited work.  These denominators include authors without ORCIDs.  A resolved work citation contributes
\begin{equation}
  \frac{1}{n_c n_a}
\end{equation}
to each identified citing-author/identified cited-author combination.  Rows with an unidentified endpoint do not produce analytic author edges; in particular, missing identifiers cannot become a shared pseudo-author.  Multiple matched authors on one citing work do not multiply the underlying resolved work reference.

Every analytic edge is keyed by subject, citing ORCID, cited ORCID, and citation year.  Only citing works assigned to the matched author's subject contribute to that author--subject's measures.  Duplicate rows are aggregated so that this key is unique.

For coauthor classification, let $c_{ij}$ be the first year in which authors $i$ and $j$ published together.  A citation from $i$ to $j$ in year $t$ is a prior-coauthor citation only if $c_{ij}<t$.  A sensitivity analysis uses $c_{ij}\leq t$ to show the effect of admitting same-year collaborations.

\subsection{Two graph populations}

The analysis deliberately separates two graph populations.
\begin{description}
  \item[Outgoing ego layer.] For each matched author--subject $i$, this layer contains citations from $i$ to any identified author reached from an eligible citing work.  It defines self-citation, prior-coauthor citation, outgoing concentration, and annual dyadic surge share.
  \item[Matched-author induced graph.] For each subject $s$, this graph retains only nodes in the matched cohort for $s$ and the edges among them.  It defines reciprocity, local clustering, within-sample citation balance, outlier components, and tier mixing.
\end{description}
The separation prevents a metric computed on all identified recipients from being described as though it were computed only among matched authors.  Journal endogamy is computed one stage earlier on deduplicated resolved work references and does not require an identified cited-author endpoint.

\subsection{Notation, metrics, and missing values}

Let $x_{ijt}$ denote the fractional citation weight from author $i$ to author $j$ in year $t$, after the construction above.  Missing dyad--years are zero.  Let $w_{ij}=\sum_{t=2020}^{2024}x_{ijt}$ be cumulative weight and let $G_s$ be the matched-author induced graph for subject $s$.  Unless a row says otherwise, self-edges are excluded.

The study-specific temporal measure is
\begin{equation}
  B_i=
  \frac{\max_{j,t=2021,\ldots,2024}[x_{ijt}-x_{ij,t-1}]_+}
       {\sum_j\sum_{t=2020}^{2024}x_{ijt}}.
  \label{eq:surge-share}
\end{equation}

\section{Paired Inference}
\label{sec:paired-inference}

The four primary outcomes are prior-coauthor citation rate, reciprocity, local clustering, and outgoing HHI.  The four secondary outcomes are self-citation rate, journal endogamy, within-sample citation balance, and maximum annual dyadic surge share.  The distinction was made before interpreting the corrected analysis.

For outcome $m$, let $d_{pm}=x^{\mathrm{Case}}_{pm}-x^{\mathrm{Control}}_{pm}$ for matched pair $p$.  Only pairs for which both values are available enter that outcome.  We report the number of complete pairs, tier summaries, the median paired difference, a paired Wilcoxon signed-rank test \parencite{Wilcoxon1945}, and the matched rank-biserial effect size
\begin{equation}
  r_{\mathrm{rb}}=\frac{W^+-W^-}{W^++W^-},
\end{equation}
where $W^+$ and $W^-$ are the positive and negative signed-rank sums after omitting zero differences.  Ninety-five percent confidence intervals for the median paired difference are obtained from \num{2000} bootstrap resamples of matched pairs, preserving both members of each pair \parencite{Efron1993}.  Benjamini--Hochberg correction is applied to the Wilcoxon $p$-values separately within the four primary and four secondary families \parencite{Benjamini1995}.

As a randomization check, each observed within-pair difference is independently multiplied by $+1$ or $-1$ in \num{10000} draws, using the absolute mean paired difference as the two-sided statistic.  This sign-flip null preserves pair magnitudes and replaces the earlier pooled-label permutation.  Primary outcomes are also repeated for exact-$h_5$ pairs and under the same-year coauthor definition.

\section{Anomaly Screen}
\label{sec:anomaly-screen}

The anomaly analysis answers a different question from paired inference: whether profiles that are unusual relative to the Control distribution occur more often among Cases.  It is not trained to predict tier and is not evidence of intent.

An author--subject is eligible only when it has at least one identified non-self outgoing citation.  The detector uses five features: self-citation rate, prior-coauthor citation rate, reciprocity, local clustering, and outgoing HHI.  Scoring additionally requires all five inputs to be available; citation eligibility and detector completeness are reported separately.  Unavailable feature values are not recoded as structural zeros.  Within each subject, features are centered at the median and scaled by the interquartile range among eligible detector-complete Controls, and the same Control transformation is applied to Cases.

One Isolation Forest is fitted per subject on eligible detector-complete Controls only, with \texttt{n\_estimators=200}, \texttt{max\_samples=auto}, \texttt{contamination=auto}, and seed~42 \parencite{Liu2008}.  A row passes the score criterion when its anomaly score is above the 99th percentile of scores among those Controls in that subject.  It passes the cohesion confirmation when at least two of prior-coauthor citation rate, reciprocity, clustering, and outgoing HHI exceed their respective 99th percentiles in the same Control reference set.  The final flag is the intersection of these criteria and is the only flag used in enrichment, component, and figure generation.

Sensitivity analyses cross the 97.5th, 99th, and 99.5th Control-percentile cutoffs with ten fixed random seeds.  We report the proportion flagged in each tier, the Case share among flagged profiles, and Case-versus-Control enrichment.  These quantities describe an operational screen; they are not measures of classification accuracy because tier is not a ground-truth anomaly label.

Flagged nodes are placed in subject-specific matched-author induced graphs using cumulative fractional dyad weights.  Weakly connected components are summarized with node count, edge weight, weighted net flow, and highest-betweenness node.  Node roles are described neutrally as \emph{net giver}, \emph{net receiver}, or \emph{highest-betweenness node}.  A component figure is generated only when at least one component contains five or more nodes; no illustrative or synthetic network is substituted.

Finally, a $2\times2$ mixing matrix is computed by summing fractional citation weight between Case and Control nodes in the matched-author induced graphs.  Its null distribution is obtained from \num{10000} independent within-pair tier-label swaps.  This preserves the graph and matching structure while breaking the observed association between tier and node identity.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

This matched design evaluates whether citation structure is associated with an operational journal-impact stratum after conditioning on subject and recent author citation productivity.  The estimand is therefore a within-pair difference, not the effect of publishing in a particular venue.  Journal choice, collaboration, topic, and citation behavior can influence one another, and the observational design does not order those mechanisms causally.

The regenerated evidence is summarized as follows.  \PrimaryFindingText\ \AnomalyFindingText\ \ComponentFindingText\ \MixingFindingText\ The detailed estimates, intervals, and sensitivity results in Section~\ref{sec:results} govern interpretation when a short summary masks heterogeneous directions or uncertainty.

The network measures complement existing author-concentration indicators.  Evdaimon et al.\ quantify how much citation impact is supplied by a small set of citing authors and hyper-collaborators \parencite{Evdaimon2024}.  The present measures instead ask whether an author's outgoing references concentrate on prior collaborators, whether ties are reciprocated inside the matched graph, and whether local neighborhoods close.  Concentration and closure can coincide, but they need not: a narrow set of citers can be mutually disconnected, while a locally closed neighborhood can distribute weight relatively evenly.  Using both perspectives can therefore improve descriptive screening.

The unit of analysis also differs from earlier venue-level research.  Kojaku et al.\ detect anomalous groups among journals \parencite{Kojaku2021}, whereas Heneberg studies journal self-citation kinetics and stacking \parencite{heneberg2016}.  Journal-level analyses are well suited to editorial and venue flows; an author-level matched design can reveal heterogeneity within venues and can enforce temporal coauthor status.  Conversely, the present design cannot substitute for journal audits, citation-context analysis, or evidence about editorial decisions.

Several non-adversarial mechanisms could produce differences in cohesion or any observed enrichment.  Narrow specialties may have small reference communities.  Geographic and language proximity may shape both collaboration and citation, especially where research questions are locally grounded.  Stable teams naturally generate prior-coauthor references and closed neighborhoods.  Differences in journal indexing, reference deposit practices, and ORCID adoption can also change the observable graph even when underlying behavior is similar.  These explanations are alternatives to, and may coexist with, any strategic response to evaluation incentives.

The Control-referenced procedure should consequently be understood as triage.  It identifies profiles that are unusual under a declared reference distribution and adds a transparent cohesion requirement.  It does not estimate the probability of manipulation, determine whether a citation was intellectually warranted, or establish intent.  Component structure and net-flow labels are summaries of observed edges only.  Any research-integrity use would require independent validation from full texts, editorial records, author disambiguation, and domain experts.

Tier mixing is likewise descriptive.  A deviation from within-pair label swaps indicates that observed tier labels align with citation flows more than expected under that restricted null.  It does not demonstrate that tiers form separate intellectual systems, nor that venue tier caused the observed links.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Crossref reference coverage varies by publisher, journal, year, and document type.  Missing references remove edges, and missing ORCIDs remove analytic endpoints even though all author rows still enter team-size denominators.  ORCID coverage may correlate with geography, career stage, or venue.  The resulting author graph is therefore a coverage-dependent projection of the literature rather than a complete citation network.

The five broad subject categories cannot capture fine topical proximity.  The journal classifier was not independently validated against blinded human coding, and subject-specific Eigenfactor quartiles remain sensitive to the observed journal network.  The 70\% portfolio rule and deterministic ORCID sample further limit generalization.  Matching on subject-specific $h_5$ improves comparability but does not balance geography, language, institution, team structure, career age, or unmeasured research topic.

Graph metrics depend on population boundaries.  Ego-layer measures include identified recipients outside the matched cohort, whereas reciprocity, clustering, balance, components, and mixing are restricted to matched nodes.  Results from the latter should not be interpreted as full-network properties.  The five-year window also left-censors collaborations and citations; the prior-coauthor rule can misclassify collaborations first observed before 2020 but missing from the snapshot.

The anomaly thresholds and Isolation Forest define an operational screen, not a validated diagnostic.  Percentile and seed sensitivity quantify some model dependence but do not supply ground-truth labels.  Finally, structural metadata cannot reveal the textual function, scientific relevance, or motivation of a citation \parencite{Liu2024}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study provides a reproducible matched framework for comparing citation-network cohesion across journal-impact strata.  It corrects work-level deduplication and team-size denominators, uses author--subject keys, separates ego and induced graph populations, and limits inference to eight interpretable measures.  \PrimaryFindingText\ \AnomalyFindingText\ \ComponentFindingText\ \MixingFindingText\ These findings should be read as associations under incomplete bibliographic coverage.  The screening results can prioritize descriptive follow-up, but they cannot establish coordination, intent, or misconduct.

\section{Matching and Sensitivity Specifications}
\label{app:matching}

Cases and Controls require at least three eligible works and a 70\% portfolio share in the relevant journal stratum.  The existing greedy match is performed without replacement within subject.  Candidate productivity is bucketed as $b=\lfloor h_5/3\rfloor$; candidates with $|b_{\mathrm{Case}}-b_{\mathrm{Control}}|\leq1$ are eligible, and the available Control minimizing absolute $h_5$ distance is selected.  Exact-$h_5$ pairs form a sensitivity subset rather than a replacement cohort.

The paired bootstrap resamples pair identifiers with replacement.  The sign-flip test acts on complete-pair differences for one metric at a time.  Multiple-testing adjustment is not pooled across primary and secondary families.  The same-year coauthor sensitivity changes only $c_{ij}<t$ to $c_{ij}\leq t$; all other definitions and pair membership remain fixed.

\section{Anomaly-Screen Specification}
\label{app:screen}

Controls define subject-specific robust centers, scales, detector fits, score cutoffs, and univariate cohesion cutoffs.  Cases do not affect those reference quantities.  The canonical feature output retains the detector score, cohesion-confirmation indicator, citation eligibility, and final flag for each $(\mathrm{ORCID},s,\mathrm{tier})$ row.  The downstream component and mixing routines consume that final flag rather than recomputing or substituting a label.

The primary screen uses seed~42 and the 99th Control percentiles.  The threshold analysis repeats the complete fit at the 97.5th, 99th, and 99.5th percentiles for ten fixed seeds.  A row with an unavailable required detector input is not zero-imputed; it receives no detector score, cannot be flagged, and is identified by a separate detector-completeness field.

\section{Reproducibility Checks}
\label{app:checks}

The rebuild checks the following manuscript-facing invariants:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $(\mathrm{ORCID},s,\mathrm{tier})$ is unique in the feature output;
  \item every analytic edge has non-null endpoints and is unique on $(s,i,j,t)$;
  \item each per-metric complete-pair count is no greater than \MatchedPairCount;
  \item every observed surge share lies in $[0,1]$;
  \item the same final flag supplies every flag-dependent enrichment, component, and figure artifact;
  \item a component figure is absent unless a component has at least five nodes; and
  \item reported numerical values enter through generated LaTeX artifacts rather than hand-copied prose.
\end{itemize}

SQL fixtures separately test fractional weight, null endpoints, work-reference deduplication, subject retention, and prior-coauthor timing.  Python unit tests cover the metric formulas, paired inference, subject-keyed joins, weighted mixing, and flag reuse.
